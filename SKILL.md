# Traction & Leads Strategy

Turn a growth request into a decision that can be tested without mistaking activity for progress. Use **Traction** to choose where to focus and lead-generation principles to decide how the chosen route earns qualified attention.

## Establish the decision

Confirm the active product, narrow customer, problem moment, offer, funnel stage, evidence, economics, constraints, and decision requested. Prefer supplied product information and observed behavior to generic frameworks. Label material unknowns as assumptions and say what conclusion changes if each is false.

If the offer or customer is still uncertain, define a learning decision and an observable market signal. Otherwise set one quantitative, time-bounded traction goal that would change the business state.

## Diagnose before prescribing

Map the path from reach through engagement, qualification, purchase, activation, retention, and referral. Name one binding constraint and no more than two supporting constraints. Do not prescribe more traffic when offer fit, conversion, activation, retention, economics, or capacity is the actual constraint.

Separate confirmed facts, observations, inferences, assumptions, recommendations, and unresolved questions. Verify current platform rules, costs, laws, and market facts from primary sources when they affect the decision.

## Select a path

- When no repeatable channel is known, use a Bullseye-style process: consider diverse routes, run small comparable tests, and focus only after a winner has evidence.
- When the channel is known but engagement is weak, improve the offer, promise, proof, qualification, or mechanism before increasing spend.
- When a route already works, expand in order: more of the proven input, better conversion at the constraint, then genuinely new placements or methods.
- Add partners, affiliates, employees, agencies, or customer referrals only when the process, economics, and operating capacity justify leverage.

For an open decision, develop two to four materially different options. For each, state the target constraint, causal mechanism, required assets, operating burden, economics, cheapest credible test, scale path, and stop condition. Recommend one and organize work into `now`, `after signal`, and `defer`.

## Make tests decisive

For every proposed test, define:

1. The hypothesis and the decision it resolves.
2. Real market contact, input volume, duration, and cost ceiling.
3. Leading indicators and business outcomes, including customer quality.
4. Success, stop, and inconclusive rules determined before results are interpreted.
5. The next action for `scale`, `repeat`, `revise`, `stop`, and `inconclusive` outcomes.

Use the user's baseline and unit economics to set thresholds. Treat book examples and industry benchmarks as prompts for investigation, not universal targets. Do not call impressions, followers, installs, or unqualified leads traction.

## Choose the smallest useful output

- **Narrow audit:** verdict; inspected evidence and coverage limits; binding constraint; prioritized fixes; validation.
- **Strategy:** goal or learning decision; confirmed context; constraint; options; recommendation; `now / after signal / defer`; test; limits.
- **Experiment:** decision; hypothesis; contact; budget and duration; metrics; success/stop/inconclusive rules; next action.
- **Result validation:** pre-set criterion; cohort and data quality; result; verdict; alternative explanations; next decision.

Strategy work does not authorize ad spend, customer contact, publishing, payments, or external sharing. Hand off execution with the confirmed customer, desired action, constraint, evidence, success metric, constraints, and unresolved facts.
